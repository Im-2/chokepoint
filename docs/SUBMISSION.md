## One-line pitch
Chokepoint finds how an attacker could reach your crown-jewel data in a graph, picks the cheapest fix, proves it on a scratch copy of the graph, and remembers what humans approve or reject.

## Short description (about 100 words)
Chokepoint is an agent over a FalkorDB graph of a cloud environment. It enumerates every attack path from the internet to the crown-jewel data, ranks the edges to cut by paths removed per unit of business disruption (so it avoids the "most paths" decoy), and proves the chosen fix by copying the graph, applying the fix to the copy and searching again: 19 paths before, 0 after. A human approves or rejects the change; rejections are stored in the graph and the planner reads them next time. Answers always carry the path, and "no path" is reported as "no modelled path", never "safe".

## Full description
**The problem.** Security teams can list permissions but struggle to answer: how could an attacker actually get to the customer database, and what is the smallest change that stops them without breaking production?

**What it does.** On a synthetic environment of 130 assets and 322 attack edges, Chokepoint:
1. Finds attack paths at any depth up to 8. At depth 2 and 3 there are none, and it says so as "no modelled path", not "safe". At depth 4 one path appears, through a forgotten service account, `svc-legacy-etl`. At depth 8 there are 19.
2. Ranks choke edges. The edge on the most paths (15) has a disruption of 9 out of 10 and is a decoy; the best edge cuts 11 paths at a disruption of 2.
3. Plans (greedy, and labelled as a heuristic, not a proof): 2 edges cut 19 of 19 paths at a total disruption of 5.
4. Proves it on a scratch graph: the graph is copied, the fix applied to the copy and the search re-run (19 to 0), then the copy is deleted.
5. Files a change for a human: an owning team found by graph traversal, a ticket and a config diff. A person approves or rejects (a rejection needs a reason).
6. Remembers. After a rejection the planner reads the decision from the graph and finds another route (for example 15 of 19 paths cut at a disruption of 6, with the 4 that remain reported honestly).

**Why a graph database.** FalkorDB is the primary database and the product cannot work without it. Path enumeration, choke-edge counting, weighted shortest paths, centrality, `GRAPH.COPY` scratch-graph simulation and the decision memory all run in the graph.

**Agent design.** The model only chooses from a fixed catalogue of nine human-written, parameterised tools served over MCP (stateless streamable HTTP); it never writes Cypher. Asset names are resolved against the graph first, and an answer checker rejects any sentence that names an asset the graph did not return or calls something safe. The whole product also works with no language model at all (a deterministic router answers from the same tools).

**Honest limits.** Synthetic data; the plan is greedy, not provably optimal; "no path" means not modelled; the public demo's decision memory is writable.
